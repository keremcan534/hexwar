    Method

    Static read of src/ plus process-isolated scenario runs through scripts/audit/harness.mjs. Seeds ix-a/b/c, 60 warm-up weeks as AI then 260 measured weeks as player, wars suppressed unless stated, lever re-applied weekly. Figures are 3-seed medians. No game code was modified.

    Budget

    The weekly closing of nation.gold and the nine dials that steer it. Budget owns almost none of the numbers it spends — it is a collection point and a rationing device. Every claim below is tied to a file, a function and a line range, and every behavioural claim to a measurement.

    Control integrity — passed

    Every dial set once at week 0 on a player nation was still exactly there 260 weeks later. The AI never touches the player's budget: every branch in runEconomicAI() 3072-3076 is gated on delegationActive, and delegation defaults to false. On an AI nation the same dials all drift to doctrine — tariff 0 → 100, admin 30 → 100, welfare 100 → 0. The one thing that does move a player's dial uninvited is politics.js applyGovernmentLimits() 206-218, which re-clamps the tariff and both army dials to the ruling party's band every week.

    The treasury binds downward only

    Holding a nation at zero gold costs it 40% of its industry (factory levels 40 → 24, GDP 792 → 597) because construction is a hard gate. But giving a nation +100,000 gold changes nothing at all — identical industry, GDP and project count. A peaceful treasury climbs to 52k–250k with no sink, and from there every dial is a preference rather than a decision. War restores the pressure, and only war: over a 50-year campaign with wars enabled, 29/29 nations borrowed and 27/29 defaulted.

    Invariant L9 — verified exact

    Δgold == ledger.net + borrowed − repaid + defaulted. Measured over 12 consecutive weeks: deviation 0.00 every single week. On the hot path the ledger is trustworthy — which makes it the right source for every number the UI shows (see Simplification, where two of the three UI balances are not).

    But L9 is asserted, not enforced. Nothing in src/ checks it; the identity lives only in ACCOUNTING_INVARIANTS.md and in scripts/audit/, which the game never runs. Four refund paths credit the treasury without booking a ledger entry, and break L9 in any week they fire: economy.js dropInvalidProjects 2100 (the state branch — the private branch three lines above is correct, so the asymmetry sits inside one if/else), recruitment.js cancelTraining 475, construction.js cancelConstruction 615-617 (its correction is clamped at 0 while projectGold is zeroed weekly at 3554), and construction.js 906-909 (no correction at all). None fired in the measured window — which is the point: the invariant holds by luck of the hot path, not by construction.

    Weekly phase order

    contacts → ai → diplomacy → reinforcements → training → command → movement
→ provinces → workers(growCities) → produce(nationBudget, gold += budget.net.gold, payTreaties)
→ economy(beginEconomy → runNationEconomy ×N → finishEconomy)
→ construction + runPolitics(applyGovernmentLimits, elections) → battles → orders
    • Trap. economy.fiscalNet is pre-tariff inside runNationEconomy and post-tariff after finishEconomy. runEconomicAI at line 3973 reads the pre-tariff value.
    • populationDemand reads class income and taxPaid from last week — fiscalBalance runs after it (documented at 2566-2569).
    • constructionUpkeep read in fiscalBalance is one week stale.
    • applyGovernmentLimits runs after the economy, so a party change re-clamps dials for next week.

    One save/load discontinuity

    city.growth is the sub-integer accumulator that turns standard of living into city population (cities.js growCities() 159-163). save.js:189-190 persists pop and pops but not growth, and the load path at :353 never restores it. Every save/load silently discards up to a full population point of accumulated growth — at the measured rate, several years of health spending on that city.

    Hidden is not dead

    dividendRevenue, shareCost, shareRevenue, borrowed, repaid, defaulted and creditPenalty have no row on the budget screen — but they are all summed into totals the player does see. They are hidden, not dead. A player who defaults sees the treasury stop falling and the interest rate climb, with no line saying why.

    Correction — protectionism does not protect domestic industry

    The slider's own note says “protects domestic industry” and the factory-level column looks like it agrees. It does not. settleGlobalTrade():3310 computes domestic = min(marketProduction, demand) before the tariff is consulted; the tariff only scales the import bid and the export surplus. Domestic producers already sell everything they can sell at home, and are paid the bare world price either way.

    Read the column again: factory levels are 40 / 39 / 39 / 40 across tariffs 0 → 100 — flat. The only outlier is the −15 row (32), the run whose treasury was drained to 2,482 at −11/week; that nation stopped building because construction is a hard gold gate. The chain is tariff → treasury → construction budget, not price protection.

    Both party tooltips are wrong too: politics.js:25-26 promise caps of 10% and 50%; fiscalPolicyLimits():195-198 enforces 25% and 100%.

    What remains is still a genuine trade-off and still the mechanic's best feature: revenue and the ability to build, against a fed population (which feeds province growth at provinces.js:796), with a party-dependent band. Keep the mechanism; fix the label.

    Subsidies — three options, one outcome

    The three policy options are bit-identical. Subsidies themselves work; the player-facing control does not.

    manual only acts on plants the player marked one-by-one; strategic (applySubsidyPolicy() 1692-1702) only marks anything while at war; none cancels a set that is already empty. The mechanism itself is real — runFactories() 2404-2410 absorbs the loss and sets profit = 0, which stops runFactoryEmployment() 2193 laying workers off — worth +12% employment and +3.8% GDP for 1.6% of the treasury. But reaching it requires exactly the per-factory micromanagement that CLAUDE.md says the delegation layer exists to remove.

    And the subsidy has an undisclosed cost

    autoUpgradeFactory():1985 is if (factory.profit <= 0) return false; — while :2409 sets a subsidised plant's profit to exactly 0. A subsidised plant can therefore never level up while the subsidy is on. The tooltip promises the state covers the losses; nothing says it also freezes the plant.

    Budget → outward

    Delegating “Budget” hands over more than the label says

    delegation.js:16-21 describes the portfolio as “class taxes, social spending and the war budget”. What runEconomicAI():3078-3092 actually enables is adjustSocialAI plus adjustFiscalAI — and adjustFiscalAI():2906 calls adjustWarFiscalAI(), which additionally moves adminFunding (:2952, :2998), every factory's subsidized flag including the player's own per-plant marks (:2945, :2986-2988, :3003-3007), the national research programme (abandonProgramme :2941) and purchased investment levels (dropInvestmentLevel :2969-2971 — it can sell off Construction Capacity and Higher Education).

    The tariff is the mirror-image surprise: it is rendered on the Budget screen but delegated by the Trade switch, so a player who holds Budget themselves can watch the tariff drift ±2 every week (adjustFiscalAI():2896) with no explanation on the screen where the slider lives.

    Do not touch

    • The tariff band and its two elasticities. The only place in the budget with a genuine strategic dilemma — revenue and industry against a fed population — and the party band makes different governments play it differently.
    • Per-class taxation. Three sliders, three distinct failure modes: starvation, demotion, class annihilation. Depth, not duplication.
    • The wages / procurement split. Proven distinct by cross-test; the “strong but unsupplied army” state only exists because they are separate.
    • settleDebt and creditPenalty. Inert in peace, decisive in war, and creditPenalty has six consumers outside economy.js.
    • updateLedger's L9 identity. Exact to 0.00 on the hot path and the only thing making the budget auditable at all. Keep the identity — but it is asserted in a markdown file, not enforced in code, and four refund paths bypass it. It deserves a runtime guard, not a rewrite.
    • hud.js stabilityWhy() 1167-1183. A model of how to expose a hidden calculation — the pattern the rest of the budget should copy.

    Mechanic X-ray · 02

    Trade

    A single anonymous world pool, cleared once a week, in which no nation ever trades with another nation. The simulation is exact and its shocks are severe. The player has no trade decision at all.

    The structural fact: there are no trade partners

    settleGlobalTrade() never names a counterparty. Every country pours unsold surplus into one bucket per good and draws from that bucket in proportion to its bid.

    crossBorderTrade = min(totalSurplus, totalBid)                 // 3351, per good
flow.exports     = surplusCol[i] * crossBorderTrade / totalSurplus  // 3376, pro rata
flow.imports     = allocCol[i]   // a weighted share of the same bucket, two-pass

    Geography does not exist in the trade path. Distance, adjacency, coastline, ports, sea lanes and blockades are absent. A landlocked inland province sells into the same bucket, at the same price, as a deep-water port. Verified by grep across src/: no embargo, trade agreement, tradeRoute, blockade, sanction or trade pact. (convoy exists, but only as CONVOY_PER_EMBARKED_REGIMENT — steamers burned by troops at sea.)

    The player makes no trade decisions

    The trade screen's only interactive element is data-trade-good (tradeScreen.js:78), handled at screens.js:2598-2601: it selects which good's dossier to display. It is a reading room, not a control panel. The single lever that changes a trade outcome is the tariff — and it is rendered on the Budget screen.

    War does not affect trade at all

    settleGlobalTrade() never reads atWar, relations or any diplomatic state — verified by grep over the whole function (3279-3422). Two nations in total war go on supplying each other's markets on completely unchanged terms. There is no blockade, no embargo, no wartime premium, and no way for a player to cut an enemy off.

    What the mechanic does extremely well

    Conservation is exact (Σ imports ≡ Σ exports, gap 0 in every run), supply shocks propagate hard enough to cost a quarter of a nation's population, and tradeLedger.js is the best information layer in the project — priceReasons() 273-327 explains a price from real state and derives the consumer breakdown from the simulation's own tables with an explicit “Other” row so the total always ties out. The Budget screen should be rebuilt to this standard.

    Volumes double while prices collapse to 12% of base

    By 1936, 34–38 of 42 goods sit pinned at the price floor and 33–39 have not moved more than 2% in sixty weeks. Consistent across all three seeds. The real economy is fine: at turn 5261 the measured nation has more factories (43 → 57/114/72), more people (1.20M → 1.64M), input fulfilment near 1.0 (from 0.695) and needsMet 0.90–0.99 (from 0.785).

    The mechanism is that the price rule has no anchor. basePrice appears in updatePrices() only as the two clamp arguments (:3623, :3634) — its only other uses anywhere are the catalogue, marketGood()'s initialisation and priceOf's fallback. Nothing pulls a price back toward base, so a price is a pure multiplicative walk that keeps going in whichever direction the imbalance last pushed it until it hits a wall and stays there. Pinning starts early — 25 of 42 goods were already at a band edge at turn 321 — and it is one-way. Two further facts make it uncorrectable: settleGlobalTrade never writes to world.market.goods (grep over 3279-3422 finds only hasPriority), so the market never learns that trade failed to clear; and everything in a week transacts at last week's price, because updatePrices runs last.

    What dies is the price system. Production grows 3.3× while demand grows 2×, S/D reaches 2.21, and updatePrices():3634 ratchets every oversupplied good down to the floor and leaves it there. The consequence runs straight into every other mechanic, because nominal value is priced off these numbers: market.totalGdp falls 90% (10,914 → 966); the nation's own economy.gdp falls 2,629 → 207 while its industry grows; and fiscalBalance():2691 builds class income from baseOutputValue = Σ price × output, so the entire tax base deflates with the price level. A player who reaches 1900 finds a large, well-fed, fully-employed economy whose every number reads as worthless.

    Three chains that are wired but do nothing

    The fertilizer → agriculture bonus is dead. rawProduction():1892-1894 computes fertilized = fertilizer.demand > 0 ? fulfilled / demand : 0, but resetNationGoodsFlow() runs eight lines earlier in the same function (3860 vs 3868) and zeroes both fields. The test is always 0 > 0, so farmBonus is always exactly 1.0 and fertilizer never raises a single unit of food. The reset even preserves the ratio the calculation needs, as flow.fulfilledShare at :1844 — rawProduction reads the wrong pair. This is the game's only industry → agriculture link.

    Cement demand has no consequence. The build queue books it weekly (3931-3932), and grep cement src/game/construction.js returns nothing. Building never slows for want of cement.

    A starved factory bids full, forever. runFactories():2359 books requested = amount × laborThroughput — labour capacity, not the input-throttled throughput one line below. A factory that never receives its input keeps bidding full demand for the rest of the game, which pins that good at the 8× ceiling permanently. That is the mirror of the floor problem, and exactly what turn 321 shows: fuel at 8× base with production 0.00 and demand 2.25, beside cement, fertilizer and explosives.

    Simultaneous world glut and national shortage

    At turn 5261 the world produces 3,432 units against 1,554 of demand — and still leaves 330 units of demand unmet. Two structural reasons, both in settleGlobalTrade: exportable surplus is scaled down by retaliation before it is offered (:3323, :3325), and there is no civilian inventory anywhere in the game, so unsold output is discarded at the end of the week. Goods are destroyed in one country in the same week another goes short of them.

    The single change with the highest leverage — an addition, not a simplification

    updatePrices():3634 has no mean-reversion term, so every price is a one-way walk into a clamp. Everything downstream of a price — the tax base (fiscalBalance:2691), factory margins, the AI's investment signal, company valuations — inherits that. If one thing in Trade is worth changing, it is this line, and it is worth measuring before and after with scratchpad/trade-runner.mjs at a 5,200-week horizon.

    The honest structural note

    Trade is currently simulation depth without player depth: an exact, conserving, shock-propagating world market that the player can observe in excellent detail and influence through exactly one slider on another screen. That is a legitimate design — a living world the player reads rather than plays — but it should be a deliberate choice, not an accident. The “protects domestic industry” label suggests it was meant to be a lever.

    Do not touch

    • The two-pass water-filling allocation (3351-3370). It is what makes Σimports ≡ Σexports exact, it carries the comment naming the audit that caught the single-pass version creating money, and it measured clean in 22 of 22 runs.
    • The shock chain. Cutting one raw good costs 28–41% of world GDP and up to a quarter of a nation's population.
    • tradeLedger.js. The best information layer in the project — and the model the Budget screen should be rebuilt to.
    • Households and factories both paying the tariff (2528, 2371). This is what keeps tariff revenue honest money rather than invented money.

    Contract

    TRADE CONTRACT

Inputs      nation.economy.tariff (owned by Budget) · what the player builds
            · priorityAccess from companies
Canonical   world.market.goods[id].{price, supply, demand, history}
            NOTE: supply and demand are WEEKLY ACCUMULATORS, not stocks
Derived     nation.economy.goodsFlow[id] · nation.economy.trade
Outputs     tariffRevenue + settlement → treasury
            inputAvailability → every factory on earth
            price → baseOutputValue → the entire tax base
            shortage → needsMet → population growth and politics

Must always be true
  Σ trade.importValue == Σ trade.exportValue            (measured 0.000000000)
  Σ trade.settlement == 0
  crossBorderTrade == min(totalSurplus, totalBid), and the two-pass
    allocation closes it exactly
  priority access reorders the queue and NEVER creates goods
  there is no civilian inventory — unsold output is discarded weekly

Others may read   world.market.goods · goodsFlow · economy.trade
Others must NOT write  market flows except through addFlow/addNationFlow
                       a price except through updatePrices
                       imports/exports/shortage except through settleGlobalTrade
Phase order   runNationEconomy accumulates → finishEconomy:
              procureStrategicGoods → refreshPriorityAccess → settleGlobalTrade
              → updateLedger → updatePrices
            Any price or availability you read is LAST week's.
